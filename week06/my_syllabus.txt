Intro to Generative AI

A ten-week, hands-on introduction to how language models work and what to build with them, taught on open models running on your own laptop. No coding or math background required.

The course follows the instructor's textbook, Illustrating Generative AI , chapter by chapter, with a hands-on lab each week. Students from every major are welcome, and no coding or math background is needed.

Tuesdays and Thursdays, 1:00–1:50 pm, in KRH 327, fall quarter 2026 (September 28 to December 11). No class on Thanksgiving, November 26.

Illustrating Generative AI by Chiké Abuah. The course follows its chapters in order. The book is posted on D2L for enrolled students as weekly readings (listed in the schedule): one chapter a week; each chapter is a runnable notebook, code and real output side by side. The readings are copyrighted and provided for your own use in this course only; please don't share, post, or distribute them.

One deck and one lab per week, released as the quarter goes. Labs are written to run on your own Ollama; each ends with reflection questions and a submission checklist.

Ollama for local models (the course uses gemma4 , qwen2.5-coder , qwen3 , deepseek-r1 , qwen3-vl , and nomic-embed-text ), uv for Python, and the book's companion library, genai , that every lab imports. Models run on the class server, ollama2.cs.wallawalla.edu .

Ten weeks, one topic each. Each week's reading is on D2L. Quizzes are on Tuesday mornings on D2L, and cover the previous week's reading and lab.

Dates are subject to change; announcements on D2L take precedence. Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26; week 9 meets on Tuesday only.

One per week, done in class and finished on your own, kept in the cs238-labs repository (clone it once, git pull each week), and submitted as a text file on D2L. Reflection questions are answered in your own words, without a model.

Ten short quizzes on D2L, ten auto-graded questions each. Quizzes 1 to 9 are on Tuesday mornings before class, cover the previous week's reading, lecture, and lab, and allow one fifteen-minute attempt. Quiz 10 covers week 10 and is an open-book quiz done as homework outside class.

Class meets twice a week and much of the work happens there. Attendance counts for 30% of the grade; let me know ahead of time when you have to miss.

Late work may be accepted, but you must make arrangements with me first. If you need to turn something in late, for any reason, email me before the deadline .

This is a course about generative AI, and using it is expected. Two lines don't move: reflection questions are yours, written without a model; and every line of code you submit is yours to explain. Say which assistant you used and what for.

Collaboration on high-level ideas and approach is encouraged; copying someone else's work is not allowed, and any collaboration must be declared at the top of the submission. Academic misconduct is handled under Walla Walla University's academic integrity policy.

Lecture days are device-free: laptops closed, phones away, unless an accommodation says otherwise. Lab days are the exception, since the labs are the point of having a laptop. The reason is other people, not you: in a controlled experiment, students who could see a classmate multitasking on a laptop scored lower on the lecture material even though they weren't using one themselves (Sana, Weston, and Cepeda, 2013), and later work found that a peer's inattention spreads to the students around them, lowering their attention, note-taking, and quiz scores, even when the inattentive student sits behind them (Forrin et al., 2021, 2026; Eder, 1982). If you need a device to learn, talk to me and it's fine.
